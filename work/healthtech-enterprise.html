The problem

Deals were won in one place and delivered in another

SDRs ran outreach in Salesforce. Qualification, prospect communication and hand-offs happened across conversations and documents that the product never saw. Once a deal closed, onboarding a new employer meant manual setup of their workforce, which took about a week per organisation.

The gaps showed up in three places: activity was hard to track, nobody could always say who owned an opportunity, and follow-up depended on memory.

The job was to give sales and delivery one connected workflow, so a prospect could move from first contact to an active, renewing customer without falling between teams.
01 · Discovery

Mapping how SDRs actually sell

I partnered directly with SDRs and GTM stakeholders to map the existing outreach, qualification, prospect communication and hand-off processes. Where the field team lost time or lost track, I wrote it down, then turned that feedback into prioritised requirements for an in-product sales workflow.

02 · Pipeline

A CRM-style lead workflow inside the product

I built an in-product lead management workflow connected to Salesforce-led outreach. SDRs create leads and move each opportunity through defined stages.

01New LeadCreated from outreach
02ContactedContacts and communications logged
03QualifiedProspect concerns recorded
04Proposal / ContractSOPs and contracts delivered
05Won / LostWon accounts move to onboarding

At every stage SDRs can log contacts and communications, record prospect concerns, keep the full interaction history and track the next action. That improved opportunity visibility, account ownership and follow-up consistency.

03 · Lifecycle

Closing the deal is the middle of the story

I connected sales progression to post-sale operations. Teams can move a prospect from outreach into in-product engagement, deliver the relevant SOPs and contracts, coordinate deal closure, and then transition the won account straight into onboarding, ongoing customer management, renewals and retention workflows.

On the commercial side, I delivered tiered subscription packaging and in-app renewal triggers that contributed to 18% higher ARPU and 28% higher paid engagement.

04 · Onboarding

From a week of manual setup to a day of self-serve

I redesigned enterprise implementation and onboarding, replacing manual setup with self-serve tools: bulk imports, data validation, workforce provisioning, plan and region assignment, employee visibility, activation and deactivation, permissions and approvals.

Implementation effort fell by 80% and onboarding time dropped from one week to one day.

I also introduced pre-upload validation and exception management, so incomplete employee records are caught before submission instead of after. Onboarding-related support tickets fell 35% across more than 50,000 employee records, and improvements to checkout and onboarding journeys cut drop-offs by 15%.

05 · Customer health

KPIs and dashboards customers actually adopted

I owned product objectives, KPI definitions and recurring performance reviews across two squads, aligning Engineering, Design, Sales, Customer Success and regional stakeholders.

I built customer-health dashboards tracking enrolment, utilisation, renewals, retention and workforce activity. The reporting was adopted by 62% of enterprise clients.

What I learned

The hand-off is the product

Most of the friction lived between teams, not inside any single screen: from outreach to qualification, from contract to onboarding, from onboarding to renewal. Mapping those hand-offs with the people doing the work, and designing the workflow around them, did more for ownership and follow-up than any individual feature.
