The problem

The product knew who was drifting away. Sales didn't.

Customers rarely churn without warning. Their usage thins out first: fewer logins, fewer projects touched, features left unopened. Here that signal lived in product analytics, while the SDRs who could act on it worked in the CRM. Without a link between the two, a quiet account could drift toward churn before anyone stepped in.

The same gap ran the other way. Without product signals in the CRM, SDRs had no reliable way to tell a high-intent trial from one that had already gone cold.

The job was to connect product behaviour to commercial action, so the right person hears about the right account while there is still time to help.
01 · Account health

An automated early-warning system for 170+ high-value accounts

I treated declining in-app engagement as the early warning signal for churn, then defined what "declining" meant. I wrote an internal engagement-retention rubric that set the expected level of activity for a healthy account, and the threshold below which someone should step in.

Monitoring runs against that rubric automatically. When an account falls below threshold, the system raises an alert and creates a CRM task for the SDR who owns that account. Nobody has to remember to check a dashboard.

The result was clearer account ownership and earlier conversations. The monitored cohort recorded zero observed churn during the tracking period.

Later I added a stricter "red tier" for the highest-value accounts: customers who were clearly engaged and then suddenly went quiet. On its first run it flagged two accounts worth about $24K a year, before either had cancelled.

02 · Qualification

Product-qualified leads, so SDRs call the right trials first

I defined product-qualified lead criteria from three kinds of signal: engagement, progress through activation milestones, and account fit. With Sales and Revenue Operations I turned those criteria into qualification logic and CRM routing, so high-intent accounts surface to SDRs as a prioritised list instead of a raw sign-up feed.

To keep all of this measurable, I built a live Retool-connected product-health and executive dashboard covering activation, milestone progression, feature adoption, DAU/WAU, account activity, churn, revenue and subscription performance. It is the view product and leadership use to spot friction and decide what goes on the roadmap next.

03 · Onboarding

Onboarding built around what the customer came to do

New users were dropped onto a dashboard and left to work out where to start. I replaced that with a five-goal onboarding router: users pick what they want to achieve, and in-app AI Playbooks walk them to it.

We A/B tested the router against the straight-to-dashboard experience. Trial-to-paid conversion rose from 35% to 48%, and first-session setup fell from about five minutes to under two. 80% of new users who take the quick tour now succeed in their first week.

For users who stalled, I added milestone-based guidance with behaviour-triggered email and in-app nudges, fired on missed milestones and on 24 to 72 hours of inactivity. Together the onboarding and lifecycle work contributed to roughly 70% lower trial churn. Trial email opens rose 20% and win-back conversion went from 8% to 12%.

04 · AI

AI insights that tell users what to do next

Analytics tools show a lot of data and very little direction. With Data Science and Engineering, I shipped an AI-powered insights experience that ranks tracked signals by estimated impact and surfaces the next-best action.

I owned the problem framing, the prioritisation, the labels users see and the success metrics, and designed the experiment. Daily active users rose 40%.

05 · Monetisation

A white-label platform built for how agencies actually work

Agencies resell the platform to their own clients, but the account model treated them like a single team. I redesigned it around agency and client workflows: parent–child account structures, configurable branding, role-based permissions, seat management and plan-based feature access.

Agency-plan adoption rose 20%, Growth-plan adoption 18%, and white-label monetisation 60%.

What I learned

A metric only matters if it reaches someone who can act

Engagement data on a dashboard does not save an account by itself. What changes the outcome is routing it: a defined threshold, a named owner and a task in the tool they already use. I now design every metric with a question attached: who sees this, and what do they do next?
